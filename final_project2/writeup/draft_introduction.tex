\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Reinforcement learning for portfolio allocation hits a striking
failure mode under realistic friction. Our prior milestone evaluated
Implicit Q-Learning on an 8-ETF universe (SPY, EEM, TLT, HYG, DBC,
GLD, UUP, SHY) at daily frequency across a 27-run sweep over expectile
$\tau$, advantage temperature $\beta$, and transaction cost $\lambda$.
Within each $\lambda$ band the per-cell seed std stayed below $0.02$,
but the mean test Sharpe collapsed monotonically: $0.94$ at
$\lambda{=}0$, $0.36$ at $\lambda{=}0.001$, $-1.98$ at $\lambda{=}0.005$
(with a $47\%$ mean drawdown). Classical equal-weight on the same
window posted Sharpe $\sim 0.95$. The gap between $+0.95$ and $-1.98$
is not a hyperparameter problem: $(\tau, \beta)$ moves the second
decimal place; $\lambda$ moves the sign. We read it as a property of
the offline-RL family on this dataset and ask whether offline-to-online
(O2O) fine-tuning with adaptive conservatism scheduling can repair the
collapse.

The hypothesis is straightforward: an offline pretrain on a diverse
4-policy mixture buffer compresses the search space; an online
fine-tune resolves the residual offline-vs-deployment distribution
shift; an adaptive conservatism coefficient gates how aggressively the
online updates can leave the offline prior. We instantiate this as
Geodesic-CQL pretrain on 2008--2020 transitions feeding a
SAC-Dirichlet fine-tune with $\beta_t = \alpha_{\mathrm{CQL}} \cdot
\sigma(\eta \cdot \mathrm{KL}(h_{\mathrm{off}} \| h_{\mathrm{on}}))$
--- a per-step CQL-penalty modulation driven by the regime-encoder KL
between offline and online observation distributions. On a 1{,}061-day
causal test window (2022-Q1 through 2026-Q1, the post-2022
stock-bond-correlation regime), this adaptive-O2O agent achieves test
Sharpe $1.762 \pm 0.154$ across three seeds. Across four experimental phases (offline baselines, online-only
baselines, four-way O2O comparison, GRPO group-size ablation),
adaptive O2O is the only RL method to beat both equal-weight ($0.953$)
and risk-parity ($1.161$) on this window.

The headline mechanism is not what we pre-registered, and surfacing
the revision properly is the second-order contribution of this paper.
The pre-registered hypothesis was that the modulated $\beta_t$ would
let the SAC fine-tune trade actively against distribution shift in the
test window: relax pessimism under in-support observations, saturate
it under shift. The Phase~2C results disconfirm that mechanism. Both
naive ($\beta_t \equiv \alpha_{\mathrm{CQL}}$) and adaptive O2O
converge to functionally static allocations on the test window; the
differential between them is not how they trade but which static
allocation the SAC fine-tune lands on. Naive's pinned conservatism
leaves the basin random across seeds; adaptive's modulated schedule
steers the same SAC dynamics from the same Geodesic-CQL pretrain to a
uniformly strong basin --- an eight-fold seed-std reduction over naive
(Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments:phase2c}). The conservatism schedule's load-bearing
function on this universe is convergence-basin steering during
fine-tune, not online responsiveness during deployment, reframing the
design target for O2O scheduling research from ``track distribution
shift'' to ``stabilize the converged fixed point of the fine-tune
dynamics.'' Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion} unpacks the implication.

The paper's other two contributions are methodological and orthogonal
to the headline empirical result. \emph{Continuous GRPO}
(Sec.~\ref{sec:method:grpo}) adapts Group Relative Policy Optimization
\citep{shao2024deepseekmath} from discrete-token LM action spaces to a
continuous Dirichlet actor on the simplex. The critic-free derivation
exploits an environmental property: next-state independence from the
agent's action (\emph{exogeneity}; Eq.~\ref{eq:exogeneity},
unit-tested at the env boundary), under which the bootstrapped
continuation cancels in within-group centering. The formulation is
sound; empirically it does not beat classical baselines on this
universe --- a separation we surface as evidence that methodological
contribution and empirical winning are independent claims.
\emph{Algorithm-class differential as a leak diagnostic}
(Appendix~\ref{app:leak}) emerged from a label leak we discovered in
the offline-RL pipeline mid-Phase-2: unconstrained $Q$-maximizing
methods read tomorrow's reward off today's observation while
behavior-anchored methods cannot, producing a $> 2\times$ Sharpe gap
that we recommend as a STOP condition for offline-RL benchmark
builders.

Three contributions, then: (1) adaptive-O2O as the only RL method we
tested to beat both classical baselines on the post-2022 window, with
the basin-selection mechanism revision as the central interpretive
finding; (2) Continuous GRPO under exogenous state transitions;
(3) the algorithm-class-differential leak diagnostic with its
feature-space-specific scope condition. Sec.~\ref{sec:related}
positions the work; Sec.~\ref{sec:method} formalizes both methods;
Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments} reports the four-phase experimental
program; Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations} discusses interpretation and
limitations.
