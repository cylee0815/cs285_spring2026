\section*{Extended Abstract}

We study reinforcement learning for portfolio allocation across eight
broad-market ETFs at daily frequency: the agent emits weights on the
simplex and the env rewards the post-cost log return $r_t = \log(1 +
w_t^\top R_{t+1} - \lambda \|w_t - w_{t-1}\|_1)$. Our milestone
established that the standard offline-RL stack collapses smoothly
under realistic transaction-cost friction on this universe:
offline-trained policies fail to reach the equal-weight baseline at
$\lambda{=}0.001$ and actively destroy capital at $\lambda{=}0.005$.
This paper asks whether offline-to-online (O2O) fine-tuning with
adaptive conservatism scheduling repairs the collapse. The headline
empirical result, on a 1{,}061-day causal test window (2022-Q1 to
2026-Q1, the post-2022 stock-bond-correlation regime), is that an
adaptive-O2O agent (Geodesic-CQL pretrain on 2008--2020 transitions,
SAC-Dirichlet fine-tune with $\beta_t = \alpha_{\mathrm{CQL}} \cdot
\sigma(\eta \cdot \mathrm{KL}(h_{\text{off}} \| h_{\text{on}}))$)
reliably converges to a near-static allocation in the top $1.25\%$ of
the 8-asset simplex on this window: across three seeds, the
lowest-performing run reaches the 98.75th percentile of $10{,}000$
random Dirichlet$(1)$ simplex draws (Sharpe $1.629$), and the mean
reaches the 99.49th (Sharpe $1.762 \pm 0.154$) --- above the
closed-form min-variance baseline (94.65th, Sharpe $1.39$) and the
equal-weight (59.56th, $0.953$) and rolling risk-parity (81.41st,
$1.161$) classical references. Adaptive O2O is the only RL
configuration in our study that places all three seeds above the
strongest classical static optimizer.

The mechanism behind that headline is not what we pre-registered. Our
pre-registered hypothesis was that the modulated $\beta_t$ would let
the SAC fine-tune trade actively in response to distribution shift in
the test window. The Phase~2C results disconfirm that mechanism. Both
naive ($\beta_t \equiv \alpha_{\mathrm{CQL}}$) and adaptive O2O
converge to functionally static allocations (per-seed turnover
$\le 2.2\!\times\!10^{-4}$ across all six runs, three orders of
magnitude below GRPO online's $0.24$); the differential between them
is not how they trade but which static allocation the SAC fine-tune
lands on. Anchored against the same simplex distribution, naive O2O's
seeds span the 0.03rd to 100th percentile (Sharpes $-0.236$, $1.062$,
$2.179$; std $1.21$) --- effectively random sampling from the full
simplex. Adaptive O2O's seeds cluster in the top $1.25\%$ (std
$0.15$): an eight-fold variance reduction in Sharpe space, and a
sharper one in percentile space. The conservatism schedule's
primary apparent effect on this universe is convergence-basin steering
during fine-tune, not online responsiveness during deployment.

The four-way O2O comparison sits inside a broader failure-mode
landscape, each admitting a specific mechanistic interpretation. (i) On a leaky
offline pipeline (in-observation reward feature, since fixed;
Appendix~\ref{app:leak}), $Q$-maximizing offline RL exploits the leak:
TD3+BC test Sharpe $6.785 \pm 0.628$, BCQ $3.905 \pm 0.232$, vs
behavior-anchored algorithms at $\sim\!0.94$. (ii) SAC-Dirichlet's
auto-temperature blows up ($\tau \sim 10^9$); the online-only policy
collapses to a near-equal-weight static allocation identical across
seeds. (iii) Online GRPO actively trades (turnover $0.24$) at net
Sharpe cost ($0.297 \pm 0.017$); even the best group-size ablation
cell ($G{=}4$, Sharpe $0.500 \pm 0.108$) lands below equal-weight.
Every RL method we evaluate either degenerates to a static allocation
or trades into the friction tax; adaptive O2O is the only
configuration that lands a static allocation reliably in the top of
the simplex distribution.

Three contributions, then. \emph{(1) Basin-selection mechanism revision.}
We reframe what the O2O conservatism schedule does on this universe:
it steers which fixed point of the SAC fine-tune dynamics the policy
converges to, rather than governing online responsiveness during
deployment. The reframing is evidenced by adaptive O2O's
percentile-anchored win across seeds (paragraph 1) together with the
disconfirmation of the pre-registered active-trading mechanism
(paragraph 2). \emph{(2) Continuous GRPO under exogenous state
transitions.} A critic-free adaptation of Group Relative Policy
Optimization to a continuous Dirichlet actor on the simplex. The
derivation exploits next-state independence from the agent's action
(\emph{exogeneity}; unit-tested at the env boundary), under which the
bootstrapped continuation cancels under within-group centering. The
formulation is sound; whether it beats classical baselines is a
separate empirical question, answered negatively on this universe.
\emph{(3) Algorithm-class-differential leak diagnostic.} A
$> 2\times$ Sharpe gap between unconstrained $Q$-max and
conservative-$Q$/behavior-anchored methods on the same dataset signals
label leakage (CQL\_vanilla as the ``no signal''/``leakage'' control
row); we propose this as a diagnostic worth checking when building
offline-RL benchmarks. The diagnostic is feature-space-specific:
on a 216-d feature space, IQL exits the equal-weight basin and ends
in active trading (Sharpe $0.40$, turnover $0.21$, vs.\ the 56-d
basin's $\sim\!0.94$), so the algorithm-class differential should be
calibrated against a basin signature on the host feature space.

The basin-selection finding suggests an underweighted axis for
conservatism-scheduling research --- seed-stability of the fine-tune's
converged fixed point under weakly identified dynamics, not just
online responsiveness to drift --- evaluated here under one universe,
one $\lambda$, one behavior mixture, one test window.
