\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Offline RL for portfolio allocation.} Reinforcement learning
for asset allocation has been explored in two waves: a value-based wave
focused on discrete buy/sell/hold actions
\citep{moody2001learning,deng2017deep}, and a more recent
continuous-allocation wave that frames the problem as a constrained
contextual bandit with a simplex action space and trains either via DDPG
\citep{jiang2017deep} or its descendants. The offline turn imports
modern offline-RL methods --- BC \citep{pomerleau1991efficient},
BCQ \citep{fujimoto2019off}, CQL \citep{kumar2020conservative},
TD3+BC \citep{fujimoto2021minimalist}, IQL \citep{kostrikov2022offline},
AWAC \citep{nair2020awac}, EDAC \citep{an2021uncertainty}, decision
transformers \citep{chen2021decision} --- onto historical-data
trajectories rolled by a behavior policy mixture. Our milestone showed
that this stack collapses smoothly under realistic transaction-cost
friction (Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}); we follow up by re-establishing the
collapse across a broader algorithm grid (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments:phase2a})
and asking whether a critic-free, friction-aware on-policy method side-steps
the pathology rather than mitigating it.

\paragraph{GRPO and the discrete-action assumption.} Group Relative
Policy Optimization \citep{shao2024deepseekmath} replaces PPO's learned
value baseline with a within-group statistic computed across $G$
sampled completions of the same prompt. The inference-time appeal is
strong --- there is no critic to maintain --- but the original
formulation depends on the language-modeling environment in two ways
that do not transfer to typical RL: actions are discrete tokens with a
finite alphabet, and ``state transitions'' are deterministic
sequence-extension. Subsequent work has applied GRPO almost exclusively
to LLM post-training pipelines (\citealp{liu2024deepseek,yu2024rlhf}, and
follow-ons), and the few continuous-action GRPO threads we are aware of
\citep[e.g.][]{li2024grpoexp} require auxiliary critics or per-task
calibration of the group-relative scale. Our continuous-GRPO formulation
does not. The cost we pay is the exogeneity precondition --- our setting
satisfies it (Eq.~\ref{eq:exogeneity}) and most other RL settings do
not. We treat that asymmetry as a design constraint, not a generality
claim, and provide unit tests that make the constraint legible to anyone
porting the method (\texttt{tests/test\_exogeneity.py}).

\paragraph{Continuous-action policy gradients under exogenous
transitions.} The exogeneity argument we use to retire the value baseline
is closely related to the contextual-bandit analyses that motivate
direct policy-gradient estimators in causal-inference settings
\citep{dudik2014doubly,joachims2018deep}: when state transitions do not
respond to the agent's action, the bootstrapped continuation cancels in
within-state action comparisons. The financial-portfolio variant is
implicit in early Q-learning portfolio papers \citep{moody2001learning},
which deliberately embedded the price process outside the agent's action
chain, but the connection to modern policy-gradient theory has not, to
our knowledge, been formalized as a critic-removal mechanism. The closest
prior work is the family of \emph{counterfactual} policy gradients
\citep{foerster2018counterfactual} for cooperative multi-agent RL: those
methods cancel the joint baseline by sampling alternative actions for one
agent under the same teammates' actions. Our group sampling at the
same state is the single-agent analogue; the cancellation reason ---
exogeneity rather than fixed-teammate marginalization --- is different,
but the algebraic shape is the same.

\paragraph{O2O conservatism scheduling.} Offline-to-online fine-tuning
has been studied extensively in robotics
\citep{nair2020awac,zheng2023adaptive,zhao2022adaptive}, where the
online phase is sample-poor and the offline prior must be defended. The
typical solution is a fixed schedule on the conservatism coefficient
(linear or cosine decay over a known online budget). Our adaptive
schedule (Sec.~\ref{sec:method:o2o}) closes the loop by gating
pessimism on a per-step regime-KL signal, which has a natural
interpretation in financial settings where regime change is the dominant
non-stationarity. The Geodesic-CQL component
\citep[Fisher-Rao penalty;][]{ma2022geodesic} contributes the offline
phase; we do not modify it, and the geodesic vs vanilla CQL ablation
(prompt-level Phase 2D.7.1) is left to a follow-up.
